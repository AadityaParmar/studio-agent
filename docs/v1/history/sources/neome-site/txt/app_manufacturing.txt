Three gaps account for most of what a production record gets wrong, and none of them is a manufacturing problem.
Unversioned
Which recipe did that batch use?
A bill of materials edited in place cannot explain a batch made before the change, which is exactly when you need it.
Unreconciled
Stock and the floor disagree
Material is consumed on the line and deducted from the ledger days later, so neither figure is trustworthy.
Unplanned
Everything is scheduled onto one line
Without capacity per work centre, orders are planned against optimism and the bottleneck is found in the queue.
Neome closes all three by versioning the recipe, deducting as you produce, and planning against real capacity.
Bills of materials
A recipe with a version, not a file that got edited
Each product carries a version-controlled bill of materials with component-level quantities, and one version is active at a time. Keeping the earlier versions is what lets a batch produced six months ago be explained, which matters for a customer complaint and for any regulated process.

Work orders and capacity
Planned against the capacity you actually have
A work order names the product, the quantity and the schedule, and moves through stages the whole plant can see. Output and rejections are recorded as they happen on the floor, so progress at three in the afternoon is a number rather than a walk down the line to look. Because work centres carry their real capacity, the schedule is checked against what the plant can do rather than against what would be convenient, which is what lets you see a bottleneck before the queue forms in front of it.

Rejections belong to the run that produced them
