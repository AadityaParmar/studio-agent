The gate is where accountability usually stops.

Three gaps account for most of what walks off a site, and none of them is a security problem.

Unapproved

It left on somebody's word

The guard was told it was fine, so material moved on a verbal instruction nobody can produce later.

Unreturned

The returnable never came back

A die went out for repair months ago, and nobody is chasing it because nobody is tracking it.

Unsearchable

The register is a book at the gate

Finding one movement means reading pages, so the log exists but nothing is ever actually looked up.

Neome closes all three by approving before the gate, keeping returnables open, and making the log something you can search.

Raising a pass

Say what is leaving, and whether it is coming back

A pass records the material, the quantity, the purpose and a photograph of the item, and its type decides what follows. Marking it returnable sets an expected return date, which is the difference between an item that is tracked and one that is simply gone.

  • Inward and outward
  • Returnable or not
  • Photo and purpose recorded

Approval

The guard checks a pass, not a person

Outward movement is approved before it reaches the gate, so security verifies a document rather than judging whether the person in front of them has the authority. Each step carries its time, which makes the sequence an audit trail rather than three separate claims.

  • Approval before movement
  • Verified at the gate
  • Time-stamped steps

Returnables

Something is chasing the item that went out for repair

Returnable passes stay open until the material comes back, with the overdue ones surfaced rather than buried. A tool sent out for repair and never returned is the most expensive kind of loss, because nobody is looking for something they do not know is missing.

  • Open until returned
  • Expected return dates
  • Overdue surfaced

Vehicles and visitors

One log that answers who is on the premises

Vehicles are recorded in and out against the pass they carry, and visitors are signed in with who they are seeing and when they arrived. A paper register at the gate cannot answer who is on site right now, which is exactly the question asked in an emergency.

  • Vehicle in and out
  • Visitor sign-in
  • Live on-site list
