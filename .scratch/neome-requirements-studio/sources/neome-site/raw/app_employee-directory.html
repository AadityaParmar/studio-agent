A directory nobody trusts is a directory nobody updates.

Three gaps account for most of why an org record goes stale, and none of them is an HR effort problem.

Scattered

The org chart is a slide from last year

Roles and reporting lines live in a deck nobody updates, so the current structure is whatever people remember.

Undated

Somebody changed role, nobody knows when

Records are overwritten, so a question about who reported to whom in March cannot be answered at all.

Misrouted

Approvals go to the wrong manager

Leave and expenses route by a structure that is out of date, so requests land with someone who left the team.

Neome closes all three by keeping one record, dating every change, and making the structure do real work.

Records

One record per person, complete enough to be useful

An employee carries a code, a department, a designation, a location and contact details, with the full profile in one place rather than spread across a payroll file, a spreadsheet and somebody's memory. It is the record every other module points at when it needs to know who someone is.

  • Employee code and department
  • Designation and contact
  • One profile, not three

Structure

A reporting line the platform actually routes by

Each person has a reporting manager, and departments group the people who work together. This is not decoration: leave requests, expense claims and approvals all follow this tree, which is what makes keeping it correct worth the effort.

  • Reporting manager on the record
  • Department grouping
  • Approvals route by it

Lifecycle

Changes carry the date they took effect

A promotion, a transfer or an exit is recorded with its effective date rather than overwriting what was there. That keeps the history intact, so the question of who held a role in March has an answer, which matters for audits and for any dispute about a decision made at the time.

  • Effective-dated changes
  • Status history retained
  • Full lifecycle visible

Joiners

A new joiner triggers the work a new joiner needs

Adding someone raises the onboarding checklist rather than relying on whoever usually remembers. Because the directory is also where the team finds each other, a new person is discoverable on their first day instead of being introduced twice and forgotten.

  • Onboarding checklist triggered
  • New joiners visible
  • Self-service lookup

Reporting

What the headcount is, and how it is changing

Set a date range and every figure below it is bounded by those dates.

Headcount

How many, and where?

Total employees by department and location, and how that has moved, which is the figure every plan starts from.

New joiners

Who has just arrived?

Recent starters with their onboarding progress, so a new person is not three weeks in before somebody notices a step was missed.

Department distribution

How is the shape changing?

Where people sit across the organisation over time, which is what makes a quietly growing department visible before a budget round.
