A second visit costs more than the first one saved.

Three gaps account for most of what a field service desk loses, and none of them is a technical skill problem.

Unprioritised

Every request is urgent to the person who raised it

Without a priority and a deadline, the loudest customer is served first rather than the one owed a response.

Unequipped

The engineer arrives without the history

Nobody told them the machine failed twice already, so the visit repeats a diagnosis that was already done.

Unclosed

The job is finished, the paperwork is not

Reports are written that evening from memory, so the record is thinner than the work and billing waits.

Neome closes all three by giving the ticket a deadline, the engineer the history, and the job a signature.

Requests

Every request arrives with a priority and a deadline

A service ticket is linked to the customer, classified by priority and category, and carries an SLA deadline from the moment it is raised. That is what lets a queue be worked in the order the business owes rather than the order the phone rang.

  • Customer-linked tickets
  • Priority and category
  • SLA deadline on creation

Dispatch

Send the engineer who can actually finish it

Assignment considers who is free, who is near and who has handled that equipment before, and the engineer receives the ticket with the asset's full history attached. Arriving informed is usually the difference between one visit and two.

  • Assignment with availability
  • History attached
  • Scheduled on the calendar

On site

The job runs from a checklist, not from recollection

The engineer works through the checklist on their phone, records what was done, attaches photographs of the fault and the repair, and logs the parts used against the job. Everything is captured where the work happened rather than reconstructed afterwards.

  • Checklist on mobile
  • Photo capture
  • Parts logged to the job

Closure

Signed off before the engineer leaves site

The customer signs on the device, which closes the ticket and releases it for billing the same day. A digital sign-off also settles the question of what was agreed on site, which is otherwise the most expensive kind of disagreement to have a month later.

  • Digital sign-off
  • Closed on site
  • Released for billing

Reporting

See the breach coming, and the engineer behind the numbers

Set a date range and every figure below it is bounded by those dates.

Approaching breach

What needs attention now?

Tickets nearing their deadline, escalated before they breach, which is the only point at which the information can still change the outcome.

Engineer performance

Who is carrying what?

Jobs closed, time on site and first-visit fix rate per engineer, read from the record rather than from an impression.

Breach analysis

Where is it going wrong?

Breaches grouped by customer, engineer and equipment type, which is the difference between blaming one person and finding the actual cause.
