An empty slot costs the same as a busy one.

Three gaps account for most of the time a booking team loses, and none of them is a demand problem.

Double-booked

Two people promised the same hour

Slots live in someone's diary, so availability is whatever the person answering the phone believes.

Forgotten

The customer simply does not turn up

Nobody reminded them, an executive travelled anyway, and the slot is gone either way.

Untracked

It moved three times, nobody knows why

Rescheduling happens over calls, so the reason and the history disappear with the conversation.

Neome closes all three by making availability real, reminders automatic, and every change a recorded one.

Booking

Book against real availability, not a guess

An appointment is linked to the customer, carries the service being delivered and lands on an executive who is actually free. Status moves through scheduled, completed, cancelled or no-show, so the diary reflects what happened rather than what was planned.

  • Customer-linked
  • Service on the booking
  • Status through to outcome

Services and slots

Each service takes the time it actually takes

A demo, a site survey and a service call are not the same length, so each service type carries its own duration and the team that handles it. The working day is divided into slots accordingly, and the holiday calendar closes days nobody should be booked into.

  • Duration per service
  • Role-based assignment
  • Holiday calendar respected

Reminders

The appointment reminds the customer, so nobody has to

A confirmation goes out on booking, a reminder the day before and another an hour ahead. That sequence is the single biggest lever on no-shows, and it costs the team nothing because it happens without anyone remembering to do it.

  • Confirmation on booking
  • Day-before and hour-before
  • Reschedule link included

Rescheduling

Moving an appointment leaves a reason behind

A reschedule records what it moved from, what it moved to and why. Both the customer and the executive are notified, and the change is logged against the booking, so a slot that has moved three times is visible as a pattern rather than a surprise.

  • Reason recorded
  • Both sides notified
  • Full reschedule history
