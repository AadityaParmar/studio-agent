A visit nobody can evidence is a visit nobody can improve.

Three gaps account for most of what a field team loses, and none of them is an effort problem.

Unplanned

The day is decided in the car

Without a plan by territory, reps visit who is nearest or friendliest rather than who is due.

Unconfirmed

The visit is reported, not evidenced

A tick in a sheet that evening is a claim, and the manager has no way to tell it from a recollection.

Unrecorded

What came out of the visit stays with the rep

The outcome lives in a notebook, so the next conversation starts from nothing and the follow-up never lands.

Neome closes all three by planning the week, evidencing the call, and capturing the outcome where it happened.

Planning

A week planned by territory, not by proximity

Visits are scheduled against customers and territories, built into daily and weekly plans and sequenced by priority. The plan detects a calendar clash while it is being made, so a rep is never expected in two places at once and discovers it on the day.

  • Territory-based scheduling
  • Daily and weekly plans
  • Clash detection

Tracking

A check-in is evidence, a tick in a sheet is a claim

Reps check in and out at the customer with the location recorded, so arrival, time on site and departure are facts rather than assertions. Managers see the day as it runs, which removes the evening round of calls asking how it went.

  • Location-verified check-in
  • Time on site recorded
  • Live status for managers

Outcomes

Record what came out of it before driving away

A structured outcome form captures what was discussed, what the customer wants and what happens next, with the follow-up dated rather than intended. Everything attaches to the customer, so the relationship reads as one thread rather than a series of unconnected calls.

  • Structured outcome forms
  • Dated next action
  • Attached to the customer

Coverage

Know which customers are not being seen

Planned against actual coverage shows where the week fell short while there is still a week left. Visit frequency per customer answers the more uncomfortable question: which accounts have not been visited at all, which is usually where the churn starts.

  • Planned against actual
  • Frequency per customer
  • Gaps visible in the week

Reporting

See the week before it is over

Set a date range and every figure below it is bounded by those dates.

Coverage by territory

Where are we thin?

Planned against completed visits for every territory, so a gap is visible while the month can still absorb it.

Rep performance

Who is doing what?

Visits completed, time on site and outcomes per rep, so coaching starts from the record rather than an impression.

Visit frequency

Who has not been seen?

How often each customer has been visited, which is the first place to look when an account goes quiet.
