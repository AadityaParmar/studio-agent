A catalog is not a list. It is what every other number depends on.

Three gaps account for most of what goes wrong downstream, and none of them is a product problem.

Duplicated

Three teams, three product lists

Sales quotes from one sheet, stores counts another, finance bills from a third, and none of them agree.

Undated

The price changed, nobody knows when

A number was edited in a spreadsheet, so no quote can be defended and no margin can be explained.

Unclassified

You cannot report on what is not filed

Without a category tree, nobody can say which line sells, so the question gets answered by feel.

Neome closes all three by making one record the source, and every change to it a dated, approved event.

Products

One record every team quotes, counts and bills from

A product carries its code, category, unit, type and whether it is a stock item, with cost and selling price on the same screen as its tax rate. Sales, stores and finance all read that record, so a quote, a stock count and an invoice cannot disagree about what a thing is or costs.

  • Code, unit and product type
  • Cost against selling price
  • Stock or non-stock

Categories

A tree that grows with the range

Products sit in a multi-level category structure with parent and child levels, so a new line is filed rather than appended. Every picker, filter and report reads the same tree, which is what makes the question of which category actually sells answerable.

  • Multi-level hierarchy
  • Parent and child mapping
  • Reports read the same tree

Pricing and tax

A price change with a date, an approver and a trail

A revision is proposed rather than typed over. It carries the effective date it starts from and waits for the approval your rules require, and the full history stays on the product. Quotes raised before the change keep the price they were raised at. The tax rate lives on the item alongside it, so every quote, order and invoice computes it the same way and nobody applies last year's number.

  • Effective-date pricing
  • Approval before it applies
  • Tax rate on the item

Migration

Bring the list you already keep

Pick your spreadsheet and map its headers onto catalog fields. Anything already named the same maps itself, mandatory fields are flagged before the import runs, and every imported row picks up the default tax rate so nothing lands half-configured.

  • Column mapping
  • Mandatory fields flagged
  • Tax applied on import

Reporting

What the catalog can tell you once it is filed properly

Set a date range and every figure below it is bounded by those dates.

Performance by category

Which part of the range sells?

Sales read against the same category tree every picker and filter uses, which is what lets you compare lines without maintaining a second classification somewhere else.

Price revision history

What did we charge, and when?

Every revision with its effective date and its approver, so a question about what a customer was quoted last year is a retrieval rather than an argument.

Active and retired lines

What is still being sold?

Activation and deactivation logged per product, so a retired line stops appearing in pickers without disappearing from the history that explains an old order.
