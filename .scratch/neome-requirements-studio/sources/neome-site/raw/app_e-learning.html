Three ways training quietly stops working

None of them is about the quality of the material. They are about consistency, timing and evidence.

Inconsistent

Training depends on who ran it that day

When induction is a conversation, the second intake gets a shorter version of it and nobody notices for a year.

Forgotten

Onboarding starts when somebody remembers

A new joiner's first course arrives whenever an email gets sent, which is usually after the week in which it would have mattered.

Unverifiable

You cannot prove who was trained

A signature on a sheet in a drawer answers no question a customer, an auditor or an incident review will ask.

Neome fixes all three by fixing the course, assigning it from the joining date, and keeping the record.

Courses

Built once, delivered the same way every time

A course is set up with its modules, its duration and its content, which can be video, a document or an assessment. That removes the variation between the induction one supervisor gives and the one another gives, and it means the night shift gets the same training as the day shift.

  • Modules and duration
  • Multi-format content
  • Certification-enabled

Assignment

Onboarding that starts on day one, not week three

New joiners are assigned their onboarding courses automatically, so the first day of training does not depend on anybody remembering. Because it is tied to joining rather than to a reminder, it works in the month when three people start at once.

  • Auto-assign on joining
  • Employee-based enrolment
  • No manual chasing

Progress

Names, not just a completion percentage

Enrolments carry status and completion per person, so a department view shows who has finished and who has not. A percentage tells you there is a problem; a list of seven names is the thing a manager can actually do something with this week.

  • Status per learner
  • Completion tracking
  • Department view

Discussion

The question gets answered where the next person will find it

Each course carries a discussion channel, so a question about a step is asked and answered against the course itself. The answer stays there for the next intake rather than disappearing into a chat that nobody can search.

  • Per-course channels
  • Peer interaction
  • Answers that persist

Reporting

What an L and D review is actually asking

Set a date range and every figure below it is bounded by those dates.

Course completion

Who is still outstanding?

Completion by course, by department and by person, so the follow-up is a short list rather than a broadcast.

Certification record

Can we prove it?

Who holds which certificate and when they earned it, which is the answer an audit or an incident review actually asks for.

Engagement

Is anybody using it?

Participation across courses and discussions, which usually tells you more about a course's quality than its completion rate does.
