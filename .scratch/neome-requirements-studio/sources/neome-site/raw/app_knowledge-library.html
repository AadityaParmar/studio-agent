A knowledge base is trusted right up until it is wrong once.

Three gaps account for most of why internal libraries get abandoned, and none of them is a writing problem.

Undocumented

The knowledge left when the person did

How something is actually done lived with one experienced colleague, and now it is being rediscovered.

Unverified

Nobody checked before it went live

An article published straight from a draft is a confident answer that may be wrong, which is worse than none.

Stale

The guidance describes last year's process

Articles are written once and never revisited, so the library is trusted right up until it is not.

Neome closes all three by reviewing before publishing, keeping the history, and writing from what readers actually searched for.

Structure

Findable, or it may as well not exist

Articles are organised into categories and written with proper formatting rather than pasted as flat text, so the library can be navigated as well as searched. A knowledge base nobody can find their way around gets used once and abandoned.

  • Category-based organisation
  • Rich formatting
  • Navigable by team and customer

Publishing

Through review, not straight to live

An article moves from draft to review to published, with an approver releasing it. Because a confident wrong answer does more harm than a missing one, the review step is the difference between a library people trust and one they check twice.

  • Draft, review, publish
  • Approver releases it
  • Controlled publishing

Versions

Keep what changed, not just what is current

Every article carries its version history, so an update does not erase what the guidance used to say. That matters when something was done the old way for a reason, and it matters more when somebody needs to know which version was live at the time.

  • Version tracking
  • Full history retained
  • What changed, recorded

Audience

One library, and nothing internal reaching a customer

Role-based access decides what each audience sees, so internal procedures, field work instructions and customer-facing how-to articles live in one library without leaking into each other. That is what makes it safe to open part of it through the portal.

  • Access by role
  • Internal and customer-facing
  • Nothing exposed by accident
