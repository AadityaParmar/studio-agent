The field is not the problem. Not knowing what happened there is.

Three gaps account for most of what a growing field operation loses, and none of them is a selling problem.

Unverified

You are told the beat was worked

Attendance and visits come back as a phone call or a message, so the day is a recollection rather than a record.

Unreconciled

Stock leaves the warehouse and disappears

What an executive carries, sells and returns is squared up at month end, long after anything can be done about it.

Unconnected

Orders, collections and complaints live apart

Each sits in a different book or chat, so nobody can see what an outlet ordered, paid and complained about together.

Neome closes all three by making one app record the day as it happens, with or without a network.

Attendance and beats

The day starts on the record, not on a phone call

Attendance opens in a time-bound window with the location verified, and closes on its own, so a morning cannot be backdated. Each executive gets the beat assigned for the day, and planned coverage sits next to actual coverage while the day is still running.

  • GPS-verified attendance
  • Daily beat assignment
  • Planned against actual coverage

Visits and complaints

Every outlet visit leaves evidence behind

Visits are GPS-tagged and captured on a form built for that role, with shelf photos and a stock check against the outlet. A quality complaint raised at the counter carries the photo and the batch number, and is tracked to closure rather than lost in a chat.

  • Role-specific visit forms
  • Photo capture
  • Complaints tracked to closure

Orders and collections

Traditional retail and modern trade, on their own workflows

A beat order at a kirana counter and a modern trade order against a purchase order are not the same transaction, so they do not share a form. Both draw on one catalog and one price list, and both route to the distributor or depot that will actually fulfil them. A payment taken against either posts straight to that customer's ledger, so the outstanding balance is live and the executive standing in the shop can see what is owed before writing the next order.

  • Retail beat orders
  • Modern trade workflow
  • Payment posts to the ledger

Executive stock

Close the gap between the warehouse and the van

An executive requests stock, a supervisor assigns it, and from that moment what they are carrying is a number you can see. Sales and returns come off it automatically, so the balance in the van is known during the month rather than reconstructed after it.

  • Request and assign flow
  • Sales and returns deducted
  • Balance per executive

Offline and adoption

A dead network stops the signal, not the day

Native Android and iOS apps record attendance, visits, orders, stock and collections with no connection at all, and upload everything the moment a bar of signal returns. The whole thing runs through a chat-style interface, which is why field teams who would not sit through training pick it up in a sitting.

  • Full offline capture
  • Automatic sync
  • Chat-style interface

Reporting

Beat coverage

Was the plan worked?

Outlets planned against outlets actually visited, per executive and per beat, which is the difference between a route plan and a route that happened.

Target achievement

Where are we against the number?

Achievement tracked against target from the orders as they are booked, so it is a running figure through the month rather than a surprise at the end of it.

Collections and outstanding

What is still owed?

Payments collected on the beat against the outstanding balance per customer, live, because the entry made at the counter is the ledger entry.
