Work raised in a chat belongs to everyone, which means nobody.

Three gaps account for most of what a busy team drops, and none of them is a capacity problem.

Unowned

The issue was raised in a group chat

Everyone saw it and nobody owns it, so the first anyone knows of it being missed is the complaint.

Untimed

Urgent means whatever the sender felt

Without a category and a clock, the queue is worked by who chased hardest rather than what is owed.

Unfinished

Closed, but the work was not done

A ticket is marked resolved while the follow-up nobody wrote down is still outstanding.

Neome closes all three by giving the issue an owner, the clock a rule, and the closure a definition.

Tickets

An issue with an owner, a category and a clock

Every ticket is linked to whoever raised it, classified by category and priority, and carries an SLA deadline from the moment it lands. That is what turns a queue from a list of complaints into something that can be worked in the order the business actually owes.

  • Customer or colleague linked
  • Category and priority
  • SLA from creation

SLA

The deadline comes from a rule, not from a mood

Response times are configured by category and priority, so a quality issue and an access request are not measured against the same clock. The rule decides the deadline, which removes the argument about whether something was urgent from the moment it was raised.

  • Configured per category
  • Priority-driven timelines
  • Monitoring and breach alerts

Tasks

Break the ticket into work somebody owns

Tasks hang off the ticket with their own owner and due date, so resolving something that takes three people is tracked as three pieces of work rather than one vague state. A ticket closes when its tasks do, which makes closure a fact rather than an opinion.

  • Ticket-linked tasks
  • Owner and due date
  • Reminders before the date

Escalation

Somebody hears about it before it breaches

A ticket approaching its deadline escalates to the manager while there is still time to act. Auto-close after the customer confirms keeps the queue honest at the other end, so open tickets are genuinely open rather than resolved ones nobody tidied.

  • Escalation before breach
  • Overdue rules
  • Auto-close on confirmation

Reporting

See the queue, and whether it is getting better

Set a date range and every figure below it is bounded by those dates.

Open and breaching

What needs attention now?

Open tickets by category and priority, with the ones breaching today separated out, so the day starts with the right list.

Resolution time

How long are we taking?

Time to first response and to resolution, by category, so a slow area is visible as a pattern rather than a feeling.

Backlog and compliance

Is it getting better?

Backlog over time against SLA compliance, which together show whether the team is keeping up or quietly falling behind.
