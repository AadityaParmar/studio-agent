Most stock problems are not shortages. They are not knowing.
Three gaps account for most of what a stockholding business loses, and none of them is a buying problem.
Unlocated
You know the total, not the place
One number across every warehouse hides the fact that the stock is in the wrong one.
Untraced
It was there last week
Goods move without an entry, so the count drifts and nobody can say which movement caused it.
Unnoticed
The stock-out arrives before the warning
Reorder levels live in a person's judgement, so a shortage is found by a customer, not a report.
Neome closes all three by holding quantity per place, making every movement an entry, and putting the threshold on the item.
Items and warehouses
A total is not the same as availability
Every item carries its category, unit, reorder level and safety stock, and its quantity is held per warehouse rather than as one figure. That is the difference between knowing you have four hundred bags and knowing whether the depot taking the order has any.

Receiving the goods is the stock entry

Transfers and adjustments
Stock cannot leave one book without entering another
A transfer moves quantity out of one warehouse and into another with a transit state in between, so nothing sits in neither. An adjustment for damage or a count difference is raised with a reason and waits for approval before it touches the ledger.

Alerts
The shortage announces itself
Reorder level and safety stock sit on the item, so falling below either raises a low-stock alert rather than waiting for someone to notice. The point is not the alert; it is that the threshold is a number the business agreed rather than a judgement made under pressure.

Stock valuation
What is tied up?
The value of what you hold, by warehouse and by category, which is the number the balance sheet asks for and the one nobody can produce from a shelf count.
Fast against slow moving
How much of it is working?
The split between stock that turns and stock that sits, so the working capital question is answered item by item rather than in aggregate.
Inventory ageing
What has sat too long?
Stock by how long it has been held, which is usually where the cash went and the first place to look when the balance and the shelf disagree.
